Lifecycle workflow

Every milestone has an owner, signal, and escalation path.

  1. 1

    Preboarding

    Worker record, documents, equipment, access, manager plan, and Day-1 readiness are prepared before joining.

  2. 2

    Day 1 and Week 1

    Orientation, role expectations, mandatory compliance, buddy connection, and task initiation establish the operating baseline.

  3. 3

    Day 30

    Role clarity and task mastery signals identify support needs before problems harden.

  4. 4

    Day 60

    Social integration and manager feedback test whether the joiner is connected to people and work.

  5. 5

    Day 90

    Overall adjustment, unresolved exceptions, and extended-onboarding decisions close the governed cycle.

Four-pillar socialization model

Adjustment is measured as more than task completion.

Role clarity

Does the joiner understand responsibilities, priorities, and what successful performance looks like?

Task mastery

Can the joiner perform core work with the expected quality, independence, and control?

Social integration

Has the joiner formed working relationships with the manager, buddy, team, and key partners?

Culture

A disclosed three-score proxy supports discussion of norms, inclusion, and expected ways of working.

Operational layer

Readiness, SLA variance, delay reasons, ageing, status, and escalations reveal execution health.

Decision layer

Leaders can focus resources on departments, owners, phases, and joiners showing the clearest risk.

Research anchor: Bauer and colleagues’ onboarding socialization evidence informs the four-pillar measurement model; the dashboard does not claim local causal validation.

Governance and implementation

The dashboard is backed by an operating system.

  • RACI: 25 lifecycle activities define who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed.
  • HRIS mapping: Frappe HR–modeled employee, onboarding, task, role, and workflow relationships support implementation discussion.
  • Role-based access: HR, managers, IT, buddies, employees, administrators, and auditors receive purpose-limited views.
  • Exception management: delay reason, SLA variance, ageing, escalation status, and next action are visible together.
  • UAT: 14 tests cover calculations, filtering, exports, detail views, fallback behavior, accessibility, and privacy controls.
  • Data boundary: every public identity and record is synthetic; the experience is not connected to a production HRIS.
Built deliverables: cohort and task datasets, templates, RACI, privacy matrix, data dictionary, UAT register, methods, dashboard, and executive deck.

Executive takeaway

Onboarding improves when ownership and adjustment evidence meet early.

What leaders can decide

Which onboarding phases, departments, or accountable roles need capacity, escalation, or process redesign.

What HR Operations can run

A consistent 90-day workflow with milestones, service levels, exception ageing, and joiner-level follow-up.

What managers can see

Readiness, role clarity, task progress, socialization signals, and specific open actions rather than a vague status.

What the model does not claim

No causal proof of retention, no automated employee decision, no diagnostic judgment, and no production deployment.

The practical advantage is traceability: each KPI can be reconciled to a record, a definition, an owner, and an action path.
