# LinkedIn Post

What if an L&D dashboard started with evidence governance—not course counts?

I built a **Skills-Based L&D Planner & Talent Growth Engine** to explore that question across a synthetic 70-person manufacturing workforce.

The build connects:

- a 20-competency ontology with O*NET 31.0 provenance;
- explicit assessment standards and proficiency gaps;
- transparent performance/potential rules for a nine-box review;
- 70 individual development plans with 30/60/90-day evidence;
- Kirkpatrick Levels 1–4 without treating completion as mastery;
- RBAC, privacy, RACI, LMS workflow, UAT, and an employee challenge route.

The governed portfolio view shows 81.4% mean mastery, 58 active IDPs, a 14-person Star Talent cohort, and a mean skill gap of -0.48. The synthetic Level 4 scenario tracks +24.6% yield, -18.2% scrap, and ₹4.8 lakhs savings.

The most important line in the project is the boundary: those operational outcomes are cohort-level and non-causal. A credible L&D product should make uncertainty and attribution limits visible, not bury them under a polished chart.

I also wrote automated acceptance controls that recompute every headline measure, validate the nine-box lookup, check foreign keys and privacy patterns, and verify that the browser embeds the same records as the CSV source.

All records are synthetic. The project is designed to demonstrate L&D operations, analytics, responsible talent practice, and implementation thinking—not to automate employment decisions.
