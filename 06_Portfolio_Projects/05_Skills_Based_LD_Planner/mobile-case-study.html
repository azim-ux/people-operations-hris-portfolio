Responsible talent operations

Development data needs the same discipline as core HR data.

  • Nine-box guardrail: placement supports calibration discussion; it is not a promotion, termination, or succession decision.
  • Evidence standard: every competency defines what observable work demonstrates proficiency.
  • IDP accountability: learner, manager, mentor, L&D, and HRIS roles are separated through a 20-point RACI.
  • Role-based access: profile, plan, assessment, and reporting permissions follow purpose and least privilege.
  • Provenance: O*NET 31.0 references are disclosed under CC BY 4.0; local competency design remains explicit.
  • Privacy: every employee identity and result is synthetic, cohort reporting is preferred, and real performance decisions are out of scope.
Built deliverables: competency, employee, and IDP datasets; ontology; Kirkpatrick model; nine-box guide; RACI; LMS workflow; privacy matrix; UAT; dashboard; and deck.

Executive takeaway

Capability planning becomes useful when evidence reaches the work.

What leaders can decide

Which capability gaps create the most operational exposure and where development capacity should go first.

What L&D can run

Targeted learning portfolios, mentor-supported IDPs, milestone reviews, and evidence collection across four levels.

What managers can coach

Specific target competencies, observed gaps, development actions, and time-bound work evidence.

What the model does not claim

No causal ROI proof, no automated talent decision, no psychometric validation, and no production deployment.

The practical advantage is a traceable chain from business capability demand to competency definition, worker gap, development action, evidence, and review.
