# LinkedIn Project Release Post

What breaks first when a hiring workflow grows from dozens of applicants to 4,000?

Usually it is not the dashboard. It is the decision architecture: unclear knockout rules, duplicate transitions, rushed panels, inaccessible assessments, inconsistent evidence, and demographic data appearing where it should not.

I upgraded my **Structured Hiring & ATS Architecture Lab** into an enterprise high-volume simulation for a fully fictional precision manufacturer.

The reconciled synthetic operating view:

- 4,000 applicants across 5 requisition families
- 986 knockout progressions and 500 fully assessed finalists
- 120 hires and 3.0% applied-to-hired conversion
- 28.5-day average time to fill
- 1,836 of 2,000 evaluation records within 48 hours (91.8%)
- 0.87 knockout-progression adverse-impact ratio

The model combines a job-related work sample, four-competency BARS interview, and job-knowledge test using a 40/40/20 composite.

The most useful control remains CAND-2026-0013. Subjective impression was 4.60/5, while the governed composite was 3.92. The +0.68 gap did not prove bias; it made a halo-effect risk reviewable and prevented intuition from becoming an invisible override.

At this scale, the fairness lesson becomes sharper. The 0.87 ratio passes the four-fifths rule of thumb, while the constructed 2×2 table produces a statistically detectable Chi-square association. Large samples can make small differences visible. That does not tell us whether a criterion is job-related, practically material, accessible, or lawful.

The build includes:

- a six-stage OpenCATS state machine with idempotent batch controls
- versioned automated knockouts with human review and appeal
- five G1–G4 job and assessment blueprints
- complete BARS anchors and independent score locking
- a 20-activity RACI and 24/48/72-hour escalation chain
- stage- and requisition-level fairness monitoring
- least-privilege RBAC, 180-day rejected-resume purge, and erasure workflow
- 14 executable UAT scenarios
- three reconciled CSVs, two interactive paginated dashboards, and a five-slide deck

Every person and event is synthetic. Demographic cohorts are isolated from decision-makers and used only for aggregate monitoring.

For Talent Acquisition and HRIS teams: which control would you test first before switching on automated knockout screening at volume?
